Being a professional developer, I've been working with Pull Requests a lot. We have a microservices architecture, so at any given day I am reviewer on several PRs, and also I have several PRs open myself, waiting for others to review. It's easy to lose track of notifications, or even of PRs themselves altogether.

To keep things under control, my tool of choice for several years was [Trailer.app](https://ptsochantaris.github.io/trailer/). It's minimal UI and it's customizability were the main reasons it was my preferred solution for years. However, this customizability also came with a good deal of complexity. I often failed to get Trailer.app to show PRs I knew I was a reviewer in. Sometimes the problem was some misconfiguration in the settings, or the notifications. Other times however, I could never resolve. Configuring Trailer.app for my setup was never easy. Furthermore, I was skeptical about why some lower-level options were necessary for me to configure. For example, why should I have to choose between v3 and v4 of the Github API? Finally, I always found it difficult to understand the difference between "Participating" and "Mine". I am sure that there is good reason for all of that. But all I wanted was the list of PRs that were waiting for my review, and the list of PRs I opened. As much as I was grateful for the service Trailer.app did to me, it was time for a change.

So I (and Claude) created [PRty time](https://github.com/fragkakis/prty-time). I heavily borrowed from my beloved Trailer.app's layout, applied my own sensible defaults, without introducing it's complexity. It's not nearly as powerful or confifurable as Trailer.app, but it's perfect for me. I've also registered it with Homebrew for people to try it out easily. You can check out it's README for installation instructions.

Happy coding!
